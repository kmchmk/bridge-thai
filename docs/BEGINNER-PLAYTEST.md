# Picnic afternoon: beginner playtest

Status: protocol ready; no human sessions have been conducted or claimed.

Invite 5–8 willing beginners to try the game on their own phones. Explain that we are testing the game, not their ability. Do not collect recordings or personal data without their agreement. Start with a fresh browser profile, not by clearing someone's existing progress.

Say only: “Make a picnic with Mali. You can stop whenever you want.” Avoid explaining the controls unless they ask. Record anonymous observations locally:

| Observation | What to note |
| --- | --- |
| Starting | Time to first meaningful action; whether help was needed |
| Audio | Did they discover preview before choosing? Any delay or failed playback? |
| Understanding | After lunch, ask “What did your words cause to happen?” |
| Recovery | After a mistake, did they understand how to continue? |
| Support | Did they find meanings? Did optional reduced support feel comfortable? |
| Mobile | Missed taps, unreadable text, scrolling problems, phone/browser |
| Pausing | Leave after one stop, reload, and ask them to continue |
| Recall | After a short unrelated activity, play one earlier phrase without revealing its meaning |
| Returning | At the ending, observe whether they choose another activity voluntarily; stopping is welcome |

Afterwards ask: “What was confusing?”, “Which moment felt rewarding?” and “What would you like to do next?” Treat an offer of another mission neutrally. Do not describe play as addictive or use streak-loss pressure.

Prioritize fixes if more than one participant needs help to start, accidentally selects while trying to listen, cannot explain a visible consequence, or cannot resume. Use recall observations to improve teaching; a small sample is not evidence of long-term learning. Report actual durations instead of assuming that every session takes five minutes. Native-speaker review remains a separate requirement for draft language content.
